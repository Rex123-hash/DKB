# Invoices, bills and notes

A tax invoice is issued by a GST-registered seller on a taxable sale and shows the GST charged separately, so a registered buyer can claim input tax credit. It must carry your GSTIN, a serial invoice number, the date, the buyer's details, item description, quantity, value, the GST rate and amount, and the total payable.

A bill of supply is used instead of a tax invoice when GST cannot be charged separately — for example by a composition dealer or for exempt goods. It looks similar but does not show a GST amount, because the customer cannot claim credit on it.

A proforma invoice is not a real bill but a quotation in invoice form, sent before a sale to tell the buyer the expected items, quantities and prices. It helps the buyer arrange payment or approval; the actual tax invoice is issued only when the sale is confirmed.

A credit note is issued when you need to reduce the value of a sale already billed — for goods returned, an overcharge, or a post-sale discount. It lowers the buyer's payable and adjusts the GST you owe. A debit note does the opposite, increasing the billed value when you undercharged.

Invoice numbering should be unique and in sequence, without gaps, for each financial year. A clean, continuous series makes returns easy to file and reassures both buyers and the tax department that no sales are hidden.

Keep a copy of every invoice and bill you issue and receive. For your sales, the invoice is proof of income; for your purchases, it is proof for input tax credit and for expenses that reduce taxable profit. A simple folder, or a photo of each bill, is enough to start.

Sending a clear PDF invoice with your shop name, logo and contact details looks professional and builds trust. Adding a UPI QR code on the invoice lets customers pay instantly and records the payment automatically, which speeds up collection of dues.
