# Income tax slabs for FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27)

The new tax regime is the default for FY 2025-26. Under it, income up to ₹4 lakh is tax-free. The slabs are: ₹4 lakh to ₹8 lakh at 5%, ₹8 lakh to ₹12 lakh at 10%, ₹12 lakh to ₹16 lakh at 15%, ₹16 lakh to ₹20 lakh at 20%, ₹20 lakh to ₹24 lakh at 25%, and above ₹24 lakh at 30%.

Under the new regime a standard deduction of ₹75,000 is available to salaried persons and pensioners. A rebate makes tax zero for income up to ₹12 lakh, so a salaried person earning up to about ₹12.75 lakh after the standard deduction pays no tax.

The old tax regime is still optional. It has a basic exemption of ₹2.5 lakh and allows deductions such as up to ₹1.5 lakh under Section 80C and up to ₹25,000 for health insurance under Section 80D. Whether the old regime is cheaper depends on how many deductions you actually claim.

A health and education cess of 4% is added on the income tax in both regimes. A small shopkeeper with few investments often finds the new regime simpler, but it is worth comparing both each year.

For a shopkeeper, income tax is on profit, not on total sales. Keeping clean records of sales, purchases and expenses lets you compute profit correctly and choose the better regime.

Source: Income Tax Department (incometax.gov.in) and Union Budget 2025-26. Figures can change each budget, so verify on the official portal.
