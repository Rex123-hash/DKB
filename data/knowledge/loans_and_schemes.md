# Loans and government schemes for small shops

Working capital is the money a shop needs for day-to-day running — buying stock, paying rent and wages — before sales come in. When your own cash is not enough, a working-capital loan or an overdraft on your current account can bridge the gap, and is repaid as sales flow in.

The MUDRA scheme (Pradhan Mantri MUDRA Yojana) gives loans to small, non-farm businesses in three categories: Shishu (up to ₹50,000), Kishore (₹50,000 to ₹5 lakh) and Tarun (₹5 lakh to ₹10 lakh). These collateral-free loans are offered through banks and finance companies to help shops start or expand.

PM SVANidhi is a scheme for street vendors that offers a small collateral-free working-capital loan, starting around ₹10,000, with higher amounts on timely repayment and incentives for digital transactions. It is aimed at helping small vendors formalise and grow.

Udyam registration is the free online registration for micro, small and medium enterprises (MSMEs). An Udyam number makes it easier to get collateral-free loans, lower interest rates, and benefits like protection against delayed payments from buyers. Most small shops qualify as micro enterprises.

A CIBIL or credit score, ranging from 300 to 900, reflects how reliably you repay loans and dues. A score above about 750 is considered good and helps you get loans faster and cheaper. Paying EMIs and bills on time, and not borrowing more than you can repay, steadily improves it.

Before taking any loan, compare the interest rate, processing fees, and total repayment, and be sure the monthly EMI fits your cash flow. Borrow for things that grow the business — stock, equipment, a better location — rather than for routine expenses you cannot otherwise cover.

Avoid informal high-interest moneylenders where possible; their rates can trap a small shop in debt. Banks, registered NBFCs, and government schemes usually offer far lower rates, and building a record with them opens the door to larger, cheaper credit later.

Keep your loan and repayment records with your other books. Clean accounts, a filed income-tax return, and a steady bank statement are exactly what lenders look for, so good bookkeeping directly improves your access to affordable finance.
