# GST basics for small shopkeepers

GST (Goods and Services Tax) is a single indirect tax on the supply of goods and services in India. It replaced older taxes like VAT and service tax. As a shopkeeper you collect GST from customers on taxable sales and pay it to the government, and you can claim credit for the GST you paid on your purchases (input tax credit).

GST registration is required when your annual turnover crosses the threshold. For most states the threshold is 40 lakh rupees for a business that supplies goods, and 20 lakh rupees for services. In some special-category states these limits are lower (20 lakh for goods, 10 lakh for services). Below the threshold registration is optional, but many shopkeepers register voluntarily to claim input tax credit and to sell to GST-registered buyers.

GSTIN is your 15-digit GST identification number. It is based on your state code and PAN. You must print your GSTIN on every GST invoice you issue. Customers who are themselves registered will ask for your GSTIN so they can claim input tax credit.

A proper GST invoice (tax invoice) must show: your shop name, address and GSTIN; a unique invoice number and date; the customer's name (and GSTIN if registered); a description of the goods, quantity and value; the GST rate and the amount of CGST, SGST or IGST charged; and the total amount payable. Keeping clean invoices makes filing returns and any future audit much easier.

The composition scheme is a simpler option for small businesses with turnover up to 1.5 crore rupees. Under it you pay GST at a low flat rate on turnover (for traders it is commonly 1 percent) and file quarterly instead of monthly. The trade-off is that you cannot collect GST separately from customers and you cannot claim input tax credit. It suits shops that sell mostly to end consumers and want less paperwork.

GST returns are periodic statements of your sales and purchases. Regular taxpayers usually file GSTR-1 (outward sales) and a summary return GSTR-3B. Composition taxpayers file a simpler quarterly statement. Filing on time avoids late fees and interest, and keeps your input tax credit chain intact.

Input tax credit (ITC) lets you reduce the GST you owe on sales by the GST you already paid on business purchases. To claim ITC the purchase must be for business use, you must have a valid tax invoice, and your supplier must have actually reported and paid that tax. This is why buying from registered, compliant suppliers matters.
