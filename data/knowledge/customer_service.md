# Customer service and shop operations

Good service is what makes a customer choose your shop over the one next door. Greeting people, knowing your stock, serving quickly, and being fair on price and weight build a reputation that brings repeat business and word-of-mouth referrals.

Handle complaints calmly and quickly. Listen fully, accept genuine mistakes, and offer a fair fix — a replacement, a refund, or an adjustment. A customer whose problem is solved well often becomes more loyal than one who never had a problem at all.

Have a simple, clear policy for returns and exchanges and tell customers about it at the time of sale. Knowing they can return a faulty item makes people more comfortable buying, while a clear policy protects you from unfair or repeated misuse.

Keep the shop clean, well-lit and organised, with popular items easy to find and prices visible. A tidy layout speeds up service, reduces mistakes, and signals quality. Placing everyday essentials within easy reach and impulse items near the counter lifts sales.

Serve fairly and honestly — correct weights, correct change, and the price you quoted. Trust is a shop's biggest asset; once customers believe you are honest, they stop checking and start recommending. A single act of cheating can undo years of goodwill.

Train anyone who helps at the counter to be polite, to record every sale and udhaar entry immediately, and to confirm digital payments before releasing goods. Consistent habits across staff keep both service and records reliable even on busy days.

Remember regulars and their preferences where you can. A small personal touch — keeping aside a regular's usual item, or a quick festival greeting — turns a transaction into a relationship, and relationships are what keep a neighbourhood shop full.
