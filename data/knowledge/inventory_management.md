# Inventory and stock management

Stock is money sitting on your shelves. Too little and you lose sales when items run out; too much and your cash is locked in goods that may not sell. Good inventory management keeps just enough of the right items moving.

A reorder level is the stock quantity at which you should place a fresh order, set high enough to cover the time the supplier takes to deliver. Knowing the reorder level for each fast-moving item means you restock before running out, especially for daily essentials customers expect to always find.

Stock turnover measures how many times you sell and replace your stock in a period. A high turnover means goods sell quickly and cash keeps cycling; a low turnover warns of slow or dead stock. Tracking turnover by item shows you which products deserve more shelf space and which to reduce.

FIFO (First In, First Out) means selling the oldest stock first. It is essential for perishables and items with expiry dates like food, medicines and cosmetics, and it reduces losses from goods going bad or out of date on the shelf.

Dead stock is goods that have not sold for a long time. It ties up money and space. Clear it with discounts, bundles, or by returning it to the supplier where possible, and learn from it so you do not over-order the same slow items again.

ABC analysis sorts stock by value and importance: a few high-value 'A' items deserve close tracking, 'B' items moderate attention, and many low-value 'C' items need only loose control. Focusing effort on the items that matter most saves time and avoids costly stock-outs.

Plan stock around seasons and festivals. Demand for sweets, gifts, clothes and decorations rises sharply before Diwali, Holi, Eid and Raksha Bandhan. Order extra and arrange cash a few weeks early so you capture the peak instead of running empty on the best selling days.

Do a physical stock count regularly and match it against your records. Differences reveal theft, breakage, wrong entries or unrecorded sales. Even a quick monthly count of your main items keeps your stock figures honest and your reorder decisions sound.

A low-stock alert — a note or app reminder when an item falls below its reorder level — turns stock management from memory into a system. It is one of the simplest ways to avoid losing a regular customer to a competitor because an everyday item was missing.
