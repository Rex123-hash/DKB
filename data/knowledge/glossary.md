# Glossary of shop, accounting and finance terms

Udhaar (credit): goods or money given to a customer now, to be paid later. It increases what the customer owes you and is recorded as a credit entry in your khata.

Khata (ledger): the running record of money owed by and to each customer or supplier, built from daily transactions sorted party by party.

Balance: the net amount for a party — total credits minus debits. A positive balance means the party still owes you; a negative balance means you owe them.

Receivables: the total money customers owe you for udhaar given out. Collecting receivables on time keeps cash flowing.

Payables: the total money you owe suppliers for stock bought on credit. Paying on agreed terms keeps supplier relationships healthy.

GSTIN: the 15-digit GST identification number of a registered business, printed on tax invoices.

Input tax credit (ITC): the GST you paid on business purchases, which you subtract from the GST you owe on sales.

HSN code: a number that classifies goods to fix the correct GST rate; SAC codes do the same for services.

Tax invoice: a GST bill that shows GST charged separately, allowing a registered buyer to claim input tax credit.

Bill of supply: a bill issued when GST is not charged separately, such as by a composition dealer or for exempt goods.

Credit note: a document that reduces the value of a sale already billed, for returns, overcharges or post-sale discounts.

Debit note: a document that increases the billed value when you undercharged the buyer.

Gross profit: sales minus only the cost of goods sold.

Net profit: gross profit minus running costs like rent, salaries and electricity — the real earning of the business.

Depreciation: spreading the cost of a long-lasting asset like a fridge or vehicle over the years it is used.

Working capital: the cash a business needs for day-to-day running — buying stock, paying rent and wages — before sales come in.

Reorder level: the stock quantity at which you should place a fresh order so you do not run out before delivery.

Stock turnover: how many times you sell and replace your stock in a period; high turnover means goods sell quickly.

Dead stock: goods that have not sold for a long time, tying up cash and shelf space.

FIFO: First In, First Out — selling the oldest stock first, important for perishable and expiry-dated goods.

MDR: Merchant Discount Rate — the small fee a bank charges on card payments; UPI is generally free.

UPI: Unified Payments Interface — instant phone-based payment to a bank account via QR or UPI ID.

CIBIL score: a credit score from 300 to 900 reflecting how reliably you repay; above ~750 is considered good.

MUDRA loan: a collateral-free government-backed loan for small non-farm businesses, in Shishu, Kishore and Tarun categories.

Udyam registration: the free online MSME registration that unlocks cheaper loans and legal protections.

Composition scheme: a simplified GST option with a low flat rate on turnover and quarterly filing, but no input tax credit.

E-way bill: an electronic document required to move goods worth over ₹50,000.

Advance tax: income tax paid in instalments during the year when the yearly liability exceeds ₹10,000.

Presumptive taxation (44AD): declaring a fixed percentage of turnover as profit without detailed books.

Aging (of receivables): sorting outstanding dues by how long they have been unpaid, to prioritise recovery.

Margin: the difference between the cost price and selling price of an item, expressed as the profit per sale.
