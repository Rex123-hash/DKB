# GST — frequently asked questions

Q: Do I need GST registration for my shop?
A: Registration is required once your annual turnover crosses the threshold — broadly ₹40 lakh for goods and ₹20 lakh for services (₹20 lakh and ₹10 lakh in special-category states). You also need it immediately if you sell across state lines or sell online, regardless of turnover.

Q: What is a GSTIN?
A: It is your 15-digit GST identification number, built from your state code and PAN. You must print it on every tax invoice, and registered buyers ask for it to claim input tax credit.

Q: What are the GST tax slabs?
A: The main slabs are 0% (exempt essentials), 5%, 12%, 18% and 28% (luxury and sin goods, sometimes with extra cess). The rate for an item depends on its HSN code and can change in GST Council meetings.

Q: What is the composition scheme?
A: It is a simpler option for businesses up to ₹1.5 crore turnover. You pay a small flat rate on turnover (commonly 1% for traders) and file quarterly, but you cannot charge GST separately or claim input tax credit.

Q: Which GST returns must I file?
A: A regular taxpayer files GSTR-1 (sales) and GSTR-3B (summary) monthly or quarterly, plus an annual GSTR-9 if large. Composition dealers file CMP-08 quarterly and GSTR-4 annually.

Q: What is input tax credit (ITC)?
A: It lets you subtract the GST you paid on business purchases from the GST you owe on sales. You need a valid tax invoice and the supplier must have actually reported the tax. ITC is blocked on personal-use items and some others.

Q: When is an e-way bill needed?
A: When moving goods worth more than ₹50,000 between locations. It is generated on the e-way bill portal before transport; missing or wrong e-way bills can lead to detention and penalties.

Q: What happens if I file GST returns late?
A: You pay a daily late fee plus interest on unpaid tax, and your buyers may be unable to claim credit on your invoices. Continued non-filing can suspend or cancel your registration, so file nil returns even with no sales.

Q: What is the difference between CGST, SGST and IGST?
A: For sales within your state, GST splits into CGST (central) and SGST (state). For sales to another state, a single IGST is charged instead. The total rate is the same either way.

Q: Do I charge GST to a customer who is not registered?
A: Yes — a registered seller charges GST on taxable sales to everyone. The unregistered customer simply cannot claim input tax credit, but you still collect and deposit the tax.

Q: What is reverse charge?
A: In certain notified cases the buyer, not the seller, must pay the GST — for example some purchases from unregistered suppliers. You deposit it yourself and may claim it as credit if eligible.

Q: Can I cancel my GST registration?
A: Yes, if you close the business or fall below the threshold you can apply to cancel it. Until it is formally cancelled you must keep filing returns.

Q: What is an HSN code?
A: It is a number that classifies goods so the correct GST rate applies. The number of HSN digits you show on invoices depends on your turnover.

Q: Is e-invoicing mandatory for me?
A: Only above a turnover limit, which has been reduced in stages (currently around ₹5 crore aggregate turnover). Check the portal to see if your turnover crosses the latest threshold.

Q: How do I correct a mistake in a past GST invoice?
A: Usually through an amendment in a later GSTR-1 return rather than editing the original invoice. For value changes, issue a credit note or debit note.
