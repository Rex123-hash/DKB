# Business structures for a shop

A sole proprietorship is the simplest structure — one owner, minimal paperwork, and the business income is taxed as the owner's personal income. Most small shops are proprietorships. The downside is unlimited liability: the owner is personally responsible for all business debts.

A partnership is owned by two or more people who share profits, losses and responsibility as set out in a partnership deed. It suits a shop run by family or friends together, but partners share unlimited liability unless it is a Limited Liability Partnership.

A Limited Liability Partnership (LLP) combines partnership flexibility with limited liability, so partners are not personally liable beyond their agreed contribution. It needs registration and more compliance than a simple partnership but protects personal assets.

A private limited company is a separate legal entity owned by shareholders, with limited liability and easier access to investment. It involves the most compliance — registration, audits, annual filings — so it suits businesses planning significant growth rather than a single small shop.

Choosing a structure depends on size, risk and growth plans. A single neighbourhood shop usually starts as a proprietorship for simplicity, and considers an LLP or company only when it grows, takes on partners, or needs outside investment and liability protection.

Whatever the structure, register for the licences your business needs — Shop and Establishment (Gumasta), GST if applicable, FSSAI for food — and keep business money in a separate account. Structure and compliance together make the business credible to banks and suppliers.

Changing structure later is possible — for example converting a proprietorship into an LLP or company — but it involves paperwork and cost. It is worth thinking about your three-to-five-year plan before deciding, though starting simple is rarely a mistake for a small shop.
