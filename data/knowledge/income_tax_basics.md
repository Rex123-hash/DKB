# Income tax and bookkeeping basics for shopkeepers

Income tax is a tax on your yearly profit, not on your total sales. Your profit is sales minus the cost of goods and genuine business expenses like rent, electricity, salaries, transport and packaging. Keeping a clear record of every sale, purchase and expense is what lets you calculate profit correctly and pay the right tax.

Even small shopkeepers benefit from filing an income tax return (ITR) every year. A filed return is proof of income that helps when applying for a loan, a credit card, a visa, or a bigger shop lease. It also avoids notices from the tax department if money is deposited in your bank account.

The presumptive taxation scheme (Section 44AD) is a simple option for small traders. If your turnover is within the prescribed limit you can declare a fixed percentage of turnover as profit (commonly 8 percent, or 6 percent for digital and bank receipts) without maintaining detailed books. This reduces paperwork for many small shops.

Keep business money separate from personal money. Use a dedicated bank account or wallet for the shop. Mixing personal and shop cash makes it very hard to know your real profit and creates problems at tax time.

Record every transaction the same day. A khata or app entry made immediately is accurate; one made from memory days later usually is not. For each entry note the date, the party, the amount, whether it was credit (udhaar given) or debit (payment received), and a short note about what it was for.

Save your purchase bills and expense receipts. They are the proof for input tax credit under GST and for expenses that reduce your taxable profit. Even a simple folder or a photo of each bill is enough to start.

Advance tax may apply if your total tax for the year is large. Instead of paying everything at year end, the tax is paid in instalments during the year. A simple monthly profit estimate helps you set aside money so the tax bill is never a shock.
