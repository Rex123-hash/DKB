# GST in depth — rates, returns, e-way bill, ITC

GST has five main tax slabs: 0% (exempt essentials like fresh fruits, vegetables, milk), 5% (common-use items), 12% and 18% (most goods and services), and 28% (luxury and sin goods, sometimes with an extra cess). The rate for any item is fixed by its HSN code. Rates can change in GST Council meetings, so confirm the current rate for your goods on the official GST portal.

HSN (Harmonized System of Nomenclature) codes classify goods, and SAC codes classify services. The number of HSN digits you must show on invoices depends on your turnover — smaller businesses show fewer digits. Correct HSN coding ensures you charge the right GST rate and avoid mismatches in returns.

Regular GST taxpayers file two main returns. GSTR-1 reports all your outward sales (invoice-wise) and is usually filed monthly or quarterly. GSTR-3B is a summary of sales, purchases, tax collected and input tax credit claimed, with the tax paid alongside it. An annual return, GSTR-9, summarises the whole year for larger taxpayers.

Composition-scheme dealers file a simpler quarterly payment statement (CMP-08) and an annual return (GSTR-4) instead of monthly returns. They pay a small flat rate on turnover — commonly 1% for traders and manufacturers, 5% for restaurants — and cannot charge GST separately or claim input tax credit.

An e-way bill is an electronic document required when moving goods worth more than ₹50,000 between locations. It records the consignment, vehicle and route, and is generated on the e-way bill portal before the goods are transported. Missing or wrong e-way bills can lead to detention of goods and penalties.

E-invoicing means generating invoices in a standard format that is validated on the government portal, which returns a unique reference number (IRN) and a QR code. It applies to businesses above a turnover limit, which has been reduced in stages over the years (currently around ₹5 crore aggregate turnover) — check whether your turnover crosses the latest threshold.

The reverse charge mechanism (RCM) makes the buyer, instead of the seller, responsible for paying GST in certain notified cases — for example some purchases from unregistered suppliers or specific services. If RCM applies, you deposit the tax yourself and may then claim it as input tax credit if eligible.

Input tax credit cannot be claimed on everything. It is blocked on certain items such as personal-use goods, most motor vehicles, and goods lost, stolen or given as free samples. Claiming ineligible credit invites interest and penalties, so match your credit to valid business purchases only.

Late filing of GST returns attracts a daily late fee plus interest on any unpaid tax, and can block your buyers from claiming credit on your invoices. Persistent non-filing can lead to suspension or cancellation of your GST registration, so file on time even in months with no sales (a nil return).

If you stop your business or fall below the threshold, you can apply to cancel your GST registration. Until it is formally cancelled you must keep filing returns. Errors in past invoices can usually be corrected through amendments in a later GSTR-1, rather than changing the original invoice.
