# Buying smart — suppliers and purchasing

Choosing reliable suppliers matters as much as choosing what to sell. A good supplier delivers on time, with consistent quality and fair prices, and is GST-registered so you can claim input tax credit. Keeping two or three options for key items protects you if one fails.

Negotiate on more than price. Better credit terms, free delivery, return of unsold stock, or a discount for bulk or prompt payment can be worth more than a small price cut. Suppliers value reliable, regular buyers, so a good payment record gives you bargaining power.

Buy in quantities that match your sales, not just to chase a bulk discount. Over-buying ties up cash and risks dead stock, especially for perishable or seasonal goods. Calculate whether the discount really beats the cost of money locked in extra stock.

Always take a proper tax invoice from registered suppliers. It is your proof for input tax credit and for expenses that reduce taxable profit, and it confirms the goods are genuine. Cash purchases without bills cost you credit and create gaps in your records.

Track purchase prices over time so you notice when a supplier's rates creep up or when a better deal appears. Your own records of what you paid, item by item, are your strongest tool in the next negotiation.

Build a fair, long-term relationship rather than squeezing every supplier on every order. Suppliers prioritise loyal customers during shortages, festival rushes and credit crunches — exactly when you need stock most.

Inspect goods on delivery and record any shortage or damage immediately, before accepting the consignment. Catching problems at delivery, with the e-way bill and invoice in hand, makes returns and adjustments far easier than complaining later.

Plan purchasing around your reorder levels and the season. Order before fast-moving items run out, stock up a few weeks ahead of festivals, and arrange the cash or supplier credit in advance so you never miss peak selling days.
