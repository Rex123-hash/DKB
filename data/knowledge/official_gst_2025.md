# GST filing dates and current rules (2025)

A regular GST taxpayer files GSTR-1 (outward sales) by the 11th of the next month, and GSTR-3B (summary with tax payment) by the 20th of the next month. Filing on time avoids late fees and interest.

Small taxpayers with annual turnover up to ₹5 crore can use the QRMP scheme (Quarterly Return, Monthly Payment). Under QRMP, GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B are filed quarterly, but tax is still paid every month using form PMT-06 by the 25th of the following month. Quarterly GSTR-1 is due by the 13th and GSTR-3B by the 22nd or 24th of the month after the quarter, depending on the state.

E-invoicing is mandatory for businesses with aggregate turnover above ₹5 crore. They must generate invoices through the Invoice Registration Portal and get a unique IRN and QR code.

The composition scheme is for small businesses with turnover up to ₹1.5 crore (₹75 lakh in Himachal Pradesh and the North-Eastern states), and up to ₹50 lakh for service providers. The flat rates are 1% for traders and manufacturers, 5% for restaurants not serving alcohol, and 6% for eligible other service providers.

Composition dealers file form CMP-08 every quarter by the 18th of the month after the quarter, and an annual return. They cannot issue a tax invoice or claim input tax credit, because they pay tax themselves instead of collecting it from customers.

Source: GST portal (gst.gov.in) and GST Council. Dates and thresholds can change, so verify on the official portal.
