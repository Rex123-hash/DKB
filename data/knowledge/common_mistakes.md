# Common mistakes shopkeepers make (and how to avoid them)

Mixing personal and business money is the most common mistake. It hides your real profit and creates confusion at tax time. Keep a separate business account and pay yourself a fixed amount, treating the shop's money as the shop's.

Not recording udhaar properly loses real money. Verbal or half-remembered credit leads to disputes and forgotten dues. Record every credit and debit the same day, with the date, amount and party, ideally in a digital khata that cannot be lost.

Giving unlimited udhaar without limits or follow-up slowly drains cash. Set a credit limit per customer, agree a due date, and send gentle reminders. Loyalty matters, but so does getting paid.

Over-buying stock to chase a discount ties up cash and creates dead stock. Buy to match your sales, and check whether the discount really beats the cost of money locked in extra inventory.

Pricing without knowing your costs means you may be selling at a loss. Cover the cost of goods plus a share of rent, wages and electricity, and know your margin on each item before discounting.

Ignoring tax and compliance until there is a problem is risky. File returns on time, keep bills, and register for what your business needs. Catching up later costs penalties and stress.

Not backing up records leaves you exposed. A torn or wet register, or a lost phone, can wipe out your accounts. Use a cloud-backed app so your records survive any single mishap.

Failing to review the numbers means problems grow unseen. A few minutes each week on sales, outstanding dues and bills due catches trouble early and guides better decisions than a general feeling about business.

Trusting payment screenshots instead of the bank credit invites fraud. Always confirm money has reached your account before releasing goods, and never share OTPs or scan a QR to "receive" money.
