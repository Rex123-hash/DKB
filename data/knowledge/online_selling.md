# Selling online and reaching more customers

Selling online lets a shop reach customers beyond its street. The simplest start is taking orders over WhatsApp from a customer list and delivering locally, with payment by UPI. This needs no platform fees and builds on relationships you already have.

Online marketplaces and delivery platforms give access to many buyers but charge commissions and have their own rules. Weigh the extra sales against the fees and effort, and start with the products that travel and sell well.

ONDC (Open Network for Digital Commerce) is a government-backed network that lets small sellers list on a shared open system rather than depend on a single large platform. It aims to give local shops a fairer, lower-cost route to online customers.

To sell online across state lines or through most platforms, GST registration is usually required regardless of turnover. Keep clear records of online sales separately, as they feed into your GST returns and income.

Good photos, clear prices, accurate stock and quick replies are what convert online interest into sales. Keep your listings updated so customers do not order items you have run out of, which damages your rating and trust.

Packaging and delivery matter as much as the product online. Reliable packing, honest delivery timelines, and an easy way to handle returns build the reviews and repeat orders that grow an online presence.

Use your existing customers to grow online: ask happy buyers to share your shop, post offers in neighbourhood groups, and send festival greetings with your catalogue. Local, trusted word-of-mouth converts better than paid ads for a small shop.

Track which online channel actually earns after fees and effort. Some shops find WhatsApp and local delivery more profitable than commission-heavy platforms, while others grow well on marketplaces — measure before committing more.
