# Stock, pricing and shop operations — frequently asked questions

Q: What is a reorder level?
A: The stock quantity at which you should place a fresh order, set high enough to cover the supplier's delivery time so you never run out of fast-moving items.

Q: What does stock turnover tell me?
A: How many times you sell and replace your stock in a period. High turnover means goods sell quickly and cash keeps cycling; low turnover warns of slow or dead stock.

Q: How do I deal with dead stock?
A: Clear it with discounts, bundles, or by returning it to the supplier where possible, and learn from it so you do not over-order the same slow items again.

Q: What is FIFO and why does it matter?
A: First In, First Out — selling the oldest stock first. It is essential for perishable and expiry-dated goods like food, medicines and cosmetics to reduce spoilage losses.

Q: How should I set my prices?
A: Cover the cost of goods plus a share of rent, wages and electricity, then check nearby shops. Stay competitive on everyday items while earning a fair margin on the rest.

Q: When should I give discounts?
A: Deliberately — for prompt payment, festivals, or bundles that clear stock. Avoid blanket discounts on items that would sell anyway, as they simply give away profit.

Q: How do I keep customers coming back?
A: Reliability, fair prices and a personal touch — remembering regulars, keeping aside their usual items, and handling complaints well. Loyalty is cheaper than finding new customers.

Q: How often should I review my shop's numbers?
A: Weekly. A few minutes on total sales, outstanding udhaar and bills due catches problems early and guides better buying, pricing and credit decisions.

Q: How do I plan stock for festivals?
A: Order extra and arrange cash a few weeks ahead, based on what your customers buy for that festival. Review afterwards to sharpen next year's ordering.

Q: What is ABC analysis of stock?
A: Sorting stock by value: a few high-value 'A' items get close tracking, 'B' items moderate attention, and many low-value 'C' items need only loose control — focusing effort where it matters.
