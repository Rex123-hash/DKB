# Staff and running the shop day to day

Hiring help makes sense when you regularly lose sales because you cannot serve everyone, or when you need time to manage stock, accounts and growth. Start with the busiest hours and grow staffing as sales justify it.

Train staff on the basics that protect the business: greet customers, record every sale and udhaar entry immediately, confirm digital payments before releasing goods, and follow the return policy. Consistent habits keep both service and records reliable on busy days.

Pay staff fairly and on time, and keep a simple record of wages — it is a genuine business expense that reduces taxable profit, and clear records avoid disputes. Treating staff well reduces turnover and the cost of constantly retraining.

Set clear roles even in a small shop: who opens and closes, who handles cash, who manages stock. Defined responsibilities reduce mistakes and make it obvious where a gap or error came from.

Separate the handling of cash from the recording of sales where you can, so no single person both takes money and writes the books unchecked. Simple separation, plus a daily cash count against the records, discourages and catches pilferage.

Keep opening and closing routines: count the cash float, check the day's takings against recorded sales, note stock that ran low, and lock up securely. A steady routine catches problems daily instead of letting them pile up.

Look after staff safety and customer comfort — clear aisles, safe storage, working lights and fans, and a clean counter. A safe, pleasant shop keeps both staff and customers coming back.
