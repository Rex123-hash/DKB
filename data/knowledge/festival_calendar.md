# Festivals, seasons and demand planning

Festivals drive sharp spikes in sales for many shops, so planning stock and cash around them is one of the easiest ways to grow. Order extra and arrange money a few weeks early so you capture the peak instead of running empty on the best days.

Diwali is the biggest retail season for most shops, lifting demand for sweets, dry fruits, gifts, decorations, lights, clothes and utensils. Stock up well in advance and expect both higher footfall and more udhaar requests around it.

Holi raises demand for colours, sweets, snacks and water-based items, while Raksha Bandhan lifts rakhis, sweets and gifts. Each festival has its own product mix, so plan stock by what your customers actually buy for that occasion.

Eid brings strong demand for clothes, sweets, dry fruits and festive foods. Ramadan beforehand changes daily buying patterns, with more activity around early morning and evening — adjust stock and timings to match.

Wedding season, typically spread across cooler months, lifts demand for clothes, gifts, sweets, decorations and groceries for large gatherings. Bulk orders are common, so keep stock and credit terms ready for larger purchases.

Seasonal weather shifts demand too: cold-weather items in winter, cooling drinks and fans in summer, umbrellas and certain foods in the monsoon. Anticipating the season keeps the right goods on your shelves at the right time.

Back-to-school periods raise demand for stationery, bags and uniforms. Knowing the local school calendar lets a relevant shop stock ahead and capture a predictable yearly spike.

After every big season, review what sold and what was left over. Learning from each festival sharpens your ordering next year, so you stock more of what sells and avoid the dead stock that ties up cash.
