# Income tax — frequently asked questions

Q: Does a small shopkeeper need to file an income tax return?
A: It is strongly advisable even for small shops. A filed return is proof of income for loans, credit cards, visas and shop leases, and it avoids notices if money is deposited in your bank.

Q: What is the presumptive scheme under 44AD?
A: It lets small traders declare a fixed share of turnover as profit without detailed books — usually 8%, or 6% for digital/bank receipts — up to a turnover limit (commonly ₹2 crore, ₹3 crore if mostly non-cash).

Q: What is advance tax?
A: Income tax paid in instalments during the year instead of a lump sum, applicable when your total tax for the year is expected to exceed ₹10,000. Paying it on time avoids interest under Sections 234B and 234C.

Q: What is TDS?
A: Tax Deducted at Source — tax cut from certain payments like commission or rent before they reach you. It is deposited against your PAN, shows in Form 26AS, and can be adjusted against your final tax or refunded.

Q: When is a tax audit required?
A: When turnover crosses roughly ₹1 crore (₹10 crore if cash receipts and payments are each within 5%). Businesses correctly using the presumptive scheme are generally spared a full audit.

Q: How can I reduce my income tax legally?
A: Through deductions — Section 80C allows up to ₹1.5 lakh for PPF, life insurance, ELSS and tuition fees; 80D covers health insurance. Genuine business expenses also reduce taxable profit.

Q: Old tax regime or new — which is better?
A: The old regime has lower rates only if you claim many deductions; the new regime has lower slabs but fewer deductions. A shopkeeper with few investments often finds the new regime simpler — compare both each year.

Q: Why is PAN important?
A: PAN is your tax identity, needed to open a current account, file returns and for large transactions. Your GSTIN is also built on your PAN, linking your taxes and refunds.

Q: Is income tax charged on my total sales?
A: No — it is charged on profit, which is sales minus cost of goods and genuine business expenses like rent, salaries and transport. Good records let you compute profit correctly.

Q: What happens if cash deposits don't match my declared income?
A: They can trigger a tax notice. A consistent record of sales, a matching bank statement and a filed return together prove the deposits are genuine business income.

Q: What records should I keep for income tax?
A: Daily sales, purchase bills, expense receipts and bank statements. Even photos of bills in a folder are enough; they prove both your expenses and your input tax credit under GST.

Q: Do digital payments affect my tax?
A: They make income easy to prove and lower the presumptive profit rate to 6%. They also keep your turnover transparent, which helps with loans and avoids cash-related notices.
