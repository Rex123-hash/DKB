# Income tax in depth — TDS, advance tax, presumptive scheme, audit

The presumptive taxation scheme under Section 44AD lets small traders declare a fixed share of turnover as profit without detailed books. The usual rate is 8% of turnover, reduced to 6% for receipts that come through bank, UPI or other digital modes. It is available up to a turnover limit (commonly ₹2 crore, extended to ₹3 crore when most receipts are non-cash) — this encourages digital payments and reduces paperwork.

Advance tax is income tax paid during the year in instalments rather than as a lump sum at the end. It applies when your total tax for the year is expected to exceed ₹10,000. Estimating profit each quarter and setting aside the tax avoids interest charges for late payment under Sections 234B and 234C.

TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) means tax is cut from certain payments before they reach you — for example commission, rent, or contractor payments above set limits. The deducted amount is deposited against your PAN and shows up in your Form 26AS, which you can adjust against your final tax. If too much was deducted, you claim a refund when filing your return.

A tax audit under Section 44AB becomes mandatory when turnover crosses a threshold (broadly ₹1 crore, raised to ₹10 crore when cash receipts and payments are each within 5% of the total). Businesses using the presumptive scheme correctly are generally spared a full audit. An audit means a chartered accountant verifies and certifies your accounts.

Deductions reduce your taxable income. Section 80C allows up to ₹1.5 lakh for investments like PPF, life insurance premiums, ELSS and children's tuition fees. Other sections cover health insurance (80D), home-loan interest, and more. Keeping proof of these investments lets you legally lower your tax.

PAN (Permanent Account Number) is your unique tax identity and is required to open a current account, file returns, and for large transactions. Quoting PAN correctly links your taxes, TDS and refunds. For GST, your GSTIN itself is built on your PAN.

India has two income-tax regimes — the old regime with many deductions, and the newer regime with lower slab rates but fewer deductions. Which is cheaper depends on how many deductions you actually claim. A small shopkeeper with few investments often finds the new regime simpler, but it is worth comparing both each year.

Keep business and personal income separate and report all income honestly. Cash deposits that do not match your declared turnover can trigger notices. A consistent record of sales, a matching bank statement, and a filed return together form strong proof of genuine income.
