# Payments and banking for shops

UPI (Unified Payments Interface) lets customers pay instantly from their phone to your bank account using a QR code or your UPI ID. It is free for customers, settles money in seconds, and creates an automatic record of each payment, which makes bookkeeping and tax filing easier.

A static QR code printed at your counter can receive any amount the customer types, while a dynamic QR generated on a bill carries the exact amount. Displaying a UPI QR speeds up payment, reduces cash handling, and lowers the chance of fake notes or counting errors.

Open a separate current account for the business rather than mixing shop money with a personal savings account. A current account suits frequent transactions, supports higher limits, and keeps your business records clean for loans and tax. Most banks also link it to a payment QR and card machine.

NEFT, RTGS and IMPS are bank transfer methods. NEFT settles in batches and suits routine payments; RTGS is for large amounts and settles in real time; IMPS works instantly around the clock, including holidays. For small supplier payments, UPI or IMPS is usually fastest.

A cheque is a written instruction to your bank to pay someone. It clears in a day or two and leaves a paper trail, but can bounce if the account lacks funds — a bounced cheque is a legal offence. For reliability and speed, digital payments are now preferred over cheques for most shop dealings.

A payment gateway or card machine (POS) lets customers pay by debit or credit card. The bank charges a small fee (MDR) on card payments, though UPI is generally free. Accepting cards and UPI together means you rarely lose a sale because a customer has no cash.

Keep your bank statement matched with your shop records. Every UPI and card payment should appear in both. Regular matching catches missed entries, failed-but-recorded payments, and any unauthorised charges early, and gives you a trustworthy picture of cash flow.

Beware of payment frauds: fake "payment successful" screenshots, requests to scan a QR to receive money (scanning a QR only sends money, never receives it), and calls asking for OTPs or UPI PINs. Always confirm money has actually reached your bank account before handing over goods.
