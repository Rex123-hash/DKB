# Growing the shop — pricing, margins and marketing

Your margin is the gap between what you pay for goods and what you sell them for. Knowing the margin on each item shows where you really make money. Fast-moving items can carry a thinner margin because volume makes up for it, while slow, specialised items can carry a higher one.

Price with both cost and competition in mind. Cover your cost of goods plus a share of rent, wages and electricity, then check what nearby shops charge. Being roughly in line on everyday items while earning a fair margin on the rest keeps customers loyal without starving the business.

Use discounts deliberately, not by habit. A small discount for prompt payment, a festival offer, or a bundle of related items can lift sales and clear stock. Blanket discounts that cut into margin on items that would sell anyway simply give away profit.

Customer loyalty is built by reliability, fair prices and remembering regulars. Simple loyalty ideas — a small thank-you on repeat purchases, priority for trusted customers, or remembering a regular's usual order — cost little and bring people back, which is cheaper than finding new customers.

WhatsApp and local digital marketing are powerful and almost free. Share new stock, offers and festival greetings with a customer broadcast list, post in neighbourhood groups, and let customers order or reserve items by message. A clear shop profile with photos and timings helps people find you.

Encourage digital payments and keep a customer contact list. Phone numbers gathered with consent let you send payment reminders and offers, turning one-time buyers into repeat customers. The transaction record also tells you who your best customers are and what sells.

Review your numbers weekly — total sales, total udhaar outstanding, and which bills are due. A few minutes spent on this catches problems early and guides better buying, pricing and credit decisions than relying on a general feeling about how business is going.

Reinvest profits into what grows the shop: better-selling stock, a cleaner and brighter layout, a fridge or display that lifts sales, or a second location once the first is steady. Disciplined reinvestment, funded by collected dues and healthy margins, is how a small dukaan becomes a bigger business.

Keep a small cash buffer of one to two months of expenses. It protects the shop during slow weeks, lets you grab a good bulk-buy deal, and means a single unpaid customer or a quiet festival never threatens the basics of rent and restocking.
